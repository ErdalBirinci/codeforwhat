It is written to be read rather than to be survived, and each section states the fact it is about instead of pointing at a policy you cannot see.

4. Recipients and categories of recipients

The recipients of your data are the processors that run the service for us and the authorities entitled to ask. Each processor is bound by an Art. 28 GDPR agreement, acts only on our instructions and may not use the data for its own purposes.

  • Hosting and content delivery — GitHub Pages serves this site; the audit engine runs on Fly.io in the European Union.
  • Payments — Stripe Payments Europe processes the card and returns a payment reference to us. We never see the card number.
  • Email delivery — Resend delivers the report email and the address-verification code.
  • Authorities — tax offices for invoices, and any authority we are legally obliged to answer, which is a category of recipients rather than a named list.

We do not sell personal information, and we do not share it for cross-context behavioural advertising. See section 12 for the opt-out that exists even though there is nothing to opt out of.

5. Transfers to a third country and the safeguards

Some processors are established outside the European Economic Area, and a transfer to a third country therefore happens. It is limited to the delivery of email and to payment processing, and each route carries a documented safeguard:

  • Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs, Commission Implementing Decision (EU) 2021/914) with the transfer impact assessment required by Schrems II, for processors in the United States.
  • An adequacy decision of the European Commission where one covers the recipient's country; the EU-US Data Privacy Framework is relied on only for a recipient certified under it.

A copy of the clauses can be requested from the Data Protection Officer and is sent in redacted form where the commercial terms are confidential. No transfer is made to a country for which neither an adequacy decision nor SCCs are in place.

6. Retention period

We keep data for a defined retention period and delete it when that period ends, not "as long as necessary":

  • Audit records and reports — 24 months from delivery, so that a dossier can still be verified against its ledger. After that the record is deleted and the verification page says the record no longer exists.
  • Invoices and payment records — ten years, because § 147 of the German Fiscal Code (AO) requires it. This is the one category we cannot shorten on request.
  • Support correspondence — 12 months after the last message in the thread.
  • Server access logs — 30 days in the hosting provider's log retention, after which they are deleted by the provider.
  • Consent and interface choices — 12 months from the choice, in your browser. You can erase them at any time by clearing site data.

You have the right to access the data we hold about you and to receive a copy, the right to rectification of anything inaccurate, the right to erasure, the right to restriction of processing, the right to object, and the right to data portability of what you gave us in a structured, machine-readable format. Each right has a route and a deadline:

8. Withdrawing consent

Where processing rests on consent, you can withdraw your consent at any time, as easily as you gave it. Use the cookie settings control in the footer or the banner, or email the Data Protection Officer. Withdrawal does not affect the lawfulness of what happened before it, and it does not end a contract you have already concluded — it ends the processing that needed the consent.

9. Automated decision-making and profiling

The audit engine reaches its findings automatically, but it reaches them about a website, not about you, and no decision with legal effect is taken about a person by automated means. There is no profiling of visitors and no automated decision-making within the meaning of Art. 22 GDPR. If that changes, this section changes first.

10. Where the data comes from

Most of it comes from you: the address you type, the email you give, the messages you send. The rest is produced by the service itself. When an audit runs, the source of your data is the audited site's own public pages — the crawl reads what a visitor's browser would read and stores the evidence it found. We do not obtain data about you from data brokers or from advertising networks.

11. Whether you have to provide the data

Giving the audited hostname and an email address is required to conclude the contract: without them there is no site to audit and no address to deliver to, and the order cannot be performed. Billing details are a contractual requirement for a paid order and a statutory one for the invoice. Everything else — the optional sector selection, the language of the report, the theme — is voluntary, and declining it changes nothing about the price or the delivery.

12. Children's privacy

The service is a business tool and is not directed to children. We do not knowingly collect data from anyone under the age of 16, and if we learn that we have, we delete it. If you believe a child has given us data, write to the Data Protection Officer and it is removed.

13. Security, and what happens after a breach

Traffic is served over HTTPS with HSTS. Payment data never reaches our server. Access to the audit store is restricted to the operator, and the evidence chain is hashed so that a modified record can be detected. If a data breach occurs that is likely to result in a risk to your rights, we notify the competent supervisory authority within 72 hours of becoming aware of it, as Art. 33 GDPR requires, and we tell you without undue delay where the risk is high. Security researchers can report a vulnerability to erdalbirinci@gmail.com, which is also the contact published in the RFC 9116 security.txt at /.well-known/security.txt on this host.

A data protection impact assessment is carried out before any processing that is likely to result in a high risk, and is reviewed when the processing changes.

14. Complaints

You can lodge a complaint with a supervisory authority at any time, and you do not have to come to us first. The authority for our establishment in Germany is the Berliner Beauftragte für Datenschutz und Informationsfreiheit; the federal BfDI is competent for federal bodies, and your own national authority is competent where you live or work. Writing to the Data Protection Officer first usually resolves the matter faster, but it is not a precondition.

Your rights

To know and to access, to delete, to correct, to obtain a portable copy, to opt out of sale or sharing, to limit the use of sensitive personal information, and not to be discriminated against for exercising any of them. Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information — opening this link records an opt-out for this browser. Because no sale or sharing takes place, the practical effect is that the record is kept so that a later change cannot quietly apply to you.

Opt-out preference signals

We honour the Global Privacy Control signal. When your browser sends it, the site treats it as a valid request to opt out of sale and sharing, applies it without asking you to create an account, and records it. The same applies to a signal sent as the `Sec-GPC` header.

Appeal process

If we refuse a request, we tell you why and you have a right to appeal. Write to the Data Protection Officer with the word "appeal" in the subject; the appeal process is handled by a person who did not take the first decision, and we answer within 60 days (45 days plus a possible 60-day extension where the law allows it). If the appeal is refused, we tell you how to contact your state attorney general.

16. Email and marketing

We write to you about the order you placed. We do not add you to a newsletter for having bought an audit, and we do not send marketing email without a separate, recorded consent. Every marketing message carries a working unsubscribe link and the sender's identity and postal address, as CAN-SPAM requires, and the unsubscribe is honoured within ten business days without asking you to log in. You can also opt out of marketing at any time by writing to the Data Protection Officer.

We do not make marketing calls or send marketing texts. If that ever changes, it will be done only with your prior express written consent naming the number and the seller, and the consent will be recorded — never on the basis of a number you gave us for an order.

This notice is reviewed at least once a year and whenever the processing changes. The version and the review date are at the top of the page; a material change is announced on the site rather than slipped in, and the previous version is available on request.
